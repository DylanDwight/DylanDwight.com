Overview

Sand Dollar Restaurant & Lounge is a restaurant, bar, and live events venue in Rockaway Beach, Oregon. New competition was opening nearby. The old website wasn’t pulling its weight.

Hours & menus were out of date. The main actions were hard to find. Nothing on the site connected to ordering, reservations, or gift cards. For a restaurant that depends on visitors finding it fast, that’s a real problem. It gets worse in summer, Sand Dollar’s busiest season.

I moved the site from GoDaddy to Shift4 & redesigned it around what guests actually come to do. Then I tied it into online ordering, reservations, gift cards, and the restaurant’s Google listing. The goal was one connected system instead of a website sitting off on its own. I wanted guests to find what they need in summer so the restaurant could hold on to its regulars & long term guests instead of losing them to other businesses nearby.

UX Research & Discovery

Most people who look up a restaurant want a few things, fast: Is it open? Where is it? What’s on the menu? Can I order or book a table?

I went through the old site the way a guest would, then lined that up with the questions guests kept asking staff in person & on the phone.

What the Old Site Showed

  • The main navigation only had Home & About. The menu, reservations, and online ordering were hard to find.
  • The photos were strong, but the page didn’t put the most important guest actions first.
  • Contact details & hours sat low on the page.
  • The contact form was generic & didn’t match the decisions restaurant guests are trying to make.
  • Food, drinks, catering, and venue info existed but wasn’t organized into a clear path.

Behind the Scenes

Working with the site day to day showed what screenshots can’t. Menus & hours were outdated. Pages took a lot of scrolling. Photos didn’t always match the restaurant. There was no real support for online ordering, reservations, gift cards, or events.

The Guest Path I Designed Around

Design & Development Approach

Mobile First: That’s Where Guests Are

Lots of people look up a restaurant on their phone right before they decide where to eat, often from the beach, a hotel room, or the car. If the site is cramped or hard to use on a small screen, they move on to the next place. So I laid out every page for a phone first, then scaled it up for desktop.

  • Navigation turns into a full screen menu on mobile, led by the main guest actions.
  • Shortcuts for gift cards, reservations, and ordering sit right under the top banner.
  • The menu is real text with descriptions & prices that fits any screen size.
  • Ordering gets its own section with a direct takeout button.

Visual Direction

The look pulls from the restaurant itself: the teal logo, current food & dining photos, plus a wave divider that nods to the coast. Simple, warm, and easy to scan.

Building an Ecosystem, Not Just a Website

Most of Sand Dollar’s guest tools run on Shift4. I set them up to work as one system instead of separate tools.

The website, online ordering, Shift4 reservations, gift cards, and the POS menu all live in Shift4. Google, OpenTable, and the QR codes in the restaurant link back into it.

Not everything synced on its own. I kept hours, menus, and prices consistent by updating each place, publishing the changes, then checking the live pages.

OpenTable & Shift4 Reservations

The restaurant took bookings through both OpenTable & Shift4 reservations. I linked the two so bookings landed in the POS. Staff could see reservations alongside orders in the same system.

QR Codes in the Physical Restaurant

I made QR codes & placed them around the physical restaurant. Guests at a table or by the door could jump straight to the website, menu, or ordering on their own phone.

SEO & GEO: Getting Found

SEO helps a restaurant show up in search results. GEO (generative engine optimization) is the same idea for AI answers, like when someone asks an AI assistant “where should I eat in Rockaway Beach?”

I treated the restaurant’s info like content that has to be easy for both people & machines to read: clear, current, and the same everywhere it appears.

SEO

  • Site submission, page titles, and descriptions.
  • Location keywords & real menu content on the page.
  • Google Maps & Google Business Profile upkeep: hours, photos, plus links to the website, menu, ordering, and reservations.

GEO

  • The same name, address, phone, and hours everywhere: the site, the Google listing, the ordering pages.
  • Menus as real text, with dishes & prices that can be read & quoted.
  • Plain, descriptive copy that answers what guests ask: what it is, where, when it’s open, and what it serves.
  • A complete, current Google listing.

What People Noticed

  • Staff responded well to the new website & the cleaner POS workflow.
  • Training got easier because menus & payment steps were clearer.
  • The owner & guests gave positive feedback. Guests used the site.

Method and limitations

Source materials: Google Business Profile performance dashboards supplied by the client/project owner and the SkyTab website analytics dashboard. Google metrics cover April–September 2026, with September incomplete as of September 20, 2026. Website/SkyTab metrics use a separate latest-31-days window. Google views, searches, and interactions are platform-reported performance measures and should not be interpreted as a deduplicated, step-by-step user funnel. Online-order clicks show intent to open the order flow; they are not attributed orders or revenue. The August direction request figure comes from the same Google Business Profile records. The AI prompt results come from my own tests after the work was done, across the tools & Perplexity models listed on the SEO & GEO page, not a tracked metric, and AI answers can change over time.

Key Takeaway

A restaurant website only works when it’s connected. Google, the site, the menu, ordering, and reservations all have to tell guests the same thing, especially on a phone. People went for the menu & the website most. That backs up putting the menu, hours, and main actions first.

Reflection & Next Steps

What I Learned

The best call I made was treating the website, ordering, POS, and Google listing as one system & keeping hours & menus the same in each place. Most of the old site’s problems were really consistency problems. Staying consistent is as much an operations habit as a design choice.

Working inside the restaurant while building for it changed how I prioritize. The questions guests ask at the door or on the phone became the site structure. Next time I’d set up better tracking on day one so the impact is easier to measure.
